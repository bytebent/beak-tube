# beak-tube · research

The papers behind `design.md` and the numbers taken from them. All were read in full unless
marked otherwise.

| Source | What beak-tube takes from it |
|---|---|
| Perl et al. 2011, *Reconstruction of physiological instructions from zebra finch song*, Phys. Rev. E 84:051909, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.84.051909) | The chain: a 3.5 cm closed–open trachea (resonances 2.5 and 7.5 kHz) into a Helmholtz resonator for the OEC, glottis as its neck, beak as inertance plus radiation resistance; circuit equations (eq. 7) and values (Fig. 3 caption); OEC average near 4 kHz; bigger cavity or smaller glottis lowers the resonance. |
| Amador, Mindlin & Elemans 2025, *Transitions and tricks: nonlinear phenomena in the avian voice*, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 380:20240007, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2024.0007) | Apart from parrots, birds have an immobile tongue that does not make formants; cardinals and zebra finches tune the OEC to the fundamental; little experimental evidence that the tract changes syringeal dynamics, so source and tract can be separate plugins. |
| Beckers, Nelson & Suthers 2004, *Vocal-tract filtering by lingual articulation in a parrot*, Current Biology 14:1592–1597, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2004.08.057) | Monk parakeet tract driven by a speaker: four formants, averaged over 5 birds, near 1.3–1.9, 4.1–4.5, 5.2–7.6 and 8.6–9.9 kHz (Fig. 2). Front-to-back tongue placement moves F1 and F3 in opposite directions (F3 by about 2 kHz) and changes all amplitudes unevenly; low-to-high placement mainly attenuates all formants. Basis for Parrot mode. |
| Fitch 1999, *Acoustic exaggeration of size in birds via tracheal elongation*, J. Zool. 248:31–48, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1999.tb01020.x) | Tracheae of 25 cm (Ross's goose) to 163 cm (Japanese crane), Table 3; elongated tracheae 2–20 times longer than predicted, in at least 60 species; length does not set call pitch; formant spacing c/2L carries size, e.g. sandhill crane 406 → 313 Hz. Basis for the large end of Size and the trachea range. |
| Riede et al. 2006, *Songbirds tune their vocal tract to the fundamental frequency of their song*, PNAS 103:5543, [link](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.0601262103) | Cardinal OEC resonance follows the fundamental from below about 1.6 kHz to above about 8 kHz; volume varies several fold within a syllable; tracking of short steep sweeps is unknown. Basis for Track and Lag. Read through the research brief. |
| Research brief, *Syrinx and beak-tube: research brief for a bird-voice synthesizer pair* | Eastern towhee gape effects (a small gape cut 4–7.5 kHz; little effect below about 4–5 kHz); sparrows open the beak wider for higher notes (Westneat et al. 1993); ring dove coo about 600 Hz through an inflated esophagus with the beak closed (Fletcher et al. 2004); cassowary booms at 23–32 Hz (Mack & Jones 2003); cranes' coiled trachea driving the sternum (Gaunt et al. 1987). Secondary: read through the brief's sources, not in full. |
